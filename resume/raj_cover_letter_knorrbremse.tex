This role is about using Python and LLMs to automate manual SDLC work, which is what I have spent
most of the past year building. I am in my final year of a B.Tech in Computer Science (AI and Data
Science) at MIT-WPU in Pune, and most of my projects start from the same place: a manual engineering
workflow that I turn into a tool which runs on its own.

The project I am proudest of ran for eight months, for the Volkswagen Group Academy. Their annual
training schedule put roughly 1,243 events a year across four centres, and the whole thing was
planned by hand in spreadsheets. I led a team of three and built a Python engine for it: a pandas and
openpyxl ETL stage feeding a constraint-based allocator that resolves clashes on its own, all behind
a small Tkinter desktop app. It now places 98.5\% of events automatically and has cut the manual
effort by about 80\%. Watching those savings land for an actual automotive client is a large part of
why this position at Knorr-Bremse caught my eye.

Since then I have spent more time on the LLM side this role focuses on. In AgentForge, I connected
seven autonomous agents through a LangGraph state machine so they produce long, structured
documentation without a person in the loop. In Verita, I built a natural language to SQL generator
and an autonomous investigation agent, then put the whole platform behind an 82-test suite that runs
in CI. Between the two, I have used LLMs to generate code and documentation and written the automated
tests around them, which is both halves of what this role asks for. A third project, Nyay AI, added a
hybrid RAG pipeline on LLaMA 3.3 70B. I like owning a problem from the moment I notice the manual
effort to the day the tool that removes it ships.

I am based in Pune, so working on-site would be easy. I would genuinely enjoy bringing this kind of
automation to your engineering team. My CV is attached, and I am happy to walk through any of these
projects in more detail. Thanks for reading.
