# The Full Presentation Script
### A stage play in five acts, with the pauses written in

*Stage directions in [brackets]. The silences are as important as the words —
[PAUSE] is ~2 seconds, [BREATHE] is one slow breath, [LET THEM READ] is as long as
it takes and twice as long as comfortable.*

---

## ACT 1 — THE ENTRANCE (2 minutes)

[Walk in at 70% speed. Eyes to people, not the room. Smile at the first eye contact.]

[Place the bundle on the table — squarely, deliberately. Sit all the way back in
the chair.]

[Beat. Let them look at the bundle. Do not explain it. It is currently the most
interesting object in the room; do not rescue them from their curiosity.]

[Read the room — formal? warm? rushed? — and choose Opener A, B, or C from
`opening-monologue.md`. Deliver it. End on the line that hands control back.]

**[On handover of the bundles:]**
"I made two versions — this one's the story, this one's the engineering. They're
yours to keep either way."

[PAUSE. Say nothing while they take them.]

## ACT 2 — THE STORY (5 minutes)

[They will likely open the HR bundle. Page 1 is the movie poster. Someone will
smile. That smile is your cue, not your reward — keep going.]

"If you turn to page 2 — that comic is the honest history. Panel three is real;
week one and I were not on speaking terms."

[LET THEM READ. They are reading a comic; narrating over it murders it.]

"The short version of the story: I read your job description the way an analyst
reads data. Underneath every bullet — dashboards, models, NLP — there's one
requirement nobody wrote down: your users have to *defend* their numbers. To
auditors. To regulators. [PAUSE] Everyone's putting AI into finance right now.
Almost nobody is building the version a compliance officer could take into that
room. So that's what I built."

"It's called Verita. Every number it shows can show its work. The fraud model
tells you its honest error rate on screen. And the AI investigator—"

[BREATHE — this is the thesis sentence, do not rush it]

"—the AI investigator seals every step of its reasoning into a cryptographic
chain. If anyone alters one step afterward, the chain visibly breaks. The machine
keeps evidence against itself. That's the whole philosophy in one feature."

## ACT 3 — THE TECHNICAL WALK (8 minutes, adapt to audience)

**[WITH HR — stay at altitude. Use g2 (comic), g7 (before/after), g15 (iceberg).]**

"Page 8 has the iceberg — the demo is the tip; everything below the waterline is
what makes the tip true. The deepest part says 'the parts I didn't know I didn't
know.' That's the honest map of an intern who's been all the way down once."

[Do not go deeper than they invite. If their eyes settle on a page, go quiet.]

**[WITH TECH — open the tech bundle and go where they point. Default route:]**

"Page 2 is the architecture as a star chart — the flagship is the ringed planet:
the Investigator. Page 3 is the entire stack as a periodic table; atomic number is
adoption order, so you can read the build history off the wall chart."

[They will linger on g13 or g14. The blueprint (page 4) is for the architects:]

"The blueprint's the most truthful diagram — note the AI Annex is *restricted
access*: the LLM narrates, the engines compute, and the basement is the audit
trail, because nothing down there ever forgets. The fire exits are the global
error handler. There are no windows into the basement. That's deliberate."

[When AI usage comes up — it will:]

"Page 6, and I'd genuinely rather you read it than hear me summarize it."

[LET THEM READ the transparency page. Then, exactly:]

"Every AI output was reviewed. Every decision was mine. The judgment in this
project is human. The speed is not."

[Offer the live demo when energy peaks, not as a rescue:]

"All of it runs locally in one click — and I'd love to drop in a CSV *you*
choose, because the product has no script to fall off."

## ACT 4 — THE QUESTIONS (any length)

[This act is jazz; the war room is your sheet music. Rules of engagement:]

- [PAUSE two seconds before every answer — including the easy ones.]
- Hard technical question → "I actually thought about this — it's on page 8" →
  [turn YOUR copy, they'll turn theirs] → answer to their eyes, not the page.
- Unknown question → the first-principles script from `body-language-notes.md`.
  Never bluff. This building detects bluffing professionally.
- Ambush question → [smile first]. The smile says: I prepared for you,
  specifically. Then the defence from `ambush-questions.md`.
- [If the room goes quiet while they read — let it. Silence while they read your
  work is the sound of the interview going well.]

## ACT 5 — THE CLOSE (3 minutes)

[When the wrap-up signals come — chairs, folders, "well—"]

"Before we finish, can I ask you something?"

[PAUSE — get the nod]

**"Of all the problems Wolters Kluwer is working on right now — which one do you
personally find most interesting?"**

[Then LISTEN. Fully. Nod at content. Ask one genuine follow-up if it's there.
This is the part they'll remember: candidates talk; this one asked, then heard.]

[If — and only if — the room has laughed twice during the interview, slide the
offer letter across now:]
"One last document. This one's... aspirational."
[Let them read it. Do not explain the joke. It explains itself or it doesn't.]

[Stand WITH them. Exit line, with a real smile:]

"Thank you for the sharpest questions my work has ever gotten. Whatever you
decide — the one-pager's yours, and everything on it is checkable."

[Handshake. Name. Two seconds. Leave at 70% speed. Don't look back through the
glass — the interview ends when you're out of sight.]
