# The Close — The Move People Forget To Prepare

*Interviews are remembered by their last five minutes almost as strongly as their
first three seconds. Most candidates improvise the ending. You will not.*

---

## The last 5 minutes, scripted

**[Signal detection:** chairs shift, folders close, "well—", a glance at the
clock. The moment you detect wrap-up, stop selling. Everything from here is
landing gear.]

**Step 1 — Ask permission for one question:**
> "Before we finish — can I ask you something?"

(The permission-ask matters: it marks the question as deliberate, not filler.)

**Step 2 — THE question:**
> **"Of all the problems Wolters Kluwer is working on right now, which one do you
> personally find most interesting?"**

**Step 3 — Listen.** Fully. Take one short note if natural. Ask one genuine
follow-up ONLY if it's real curiosity, not performance. Do not connect their
answer back to your project — resist it; the pivot-to-pitch is what every other
candidate does and it retroactively cheapens the question.

**Step 4 — The exit line:**
> "Thank you for the sharpest questions my work has ever gotten. Whatever you
> decide — the one-pager is yours, and everything on it is checkable."

**Step 5 — The physical exit:** stand with them, push the chair in, handshake
(firm, two seconds, their name), leave at 70% speed, no glance back through the
glass.

---

## Why THE question works — the five things it does at once

1. **It inverts the room.** For two hours they evaluated you; now they get to be
   the interesting one. People remember conversations where they got to shine —
   and they attribute the shine to you.
2. **The word "personally" bypasses the brochure.** You'll get a real answer about
   real work — often the most honest intelligence about the team you'll receive
   all day, including whether their eyes light up at all.
3. **It signals investment maturity.** Candidates ask about hours and laptops.
   Professionals ask about problems. You've just classified yourself.
4. **It creates the dinner-table story.** "The intern asked me what *I* found
   interesting — nobody asks that." The question is engineered to be repeated, and
   every repetition is your name said warmly in a room you're not in.
5. **It gives you the perfect final beat.** Their answer ends; your thank-you
   follows naturally; the interview closes on *their* enthusiasm plus your
   composure — the exact emotional pairing you want them voting with.

---

## What NOT to do in the last five minutes

- Don't introduce new technical material. The case is closed; late evidence reads
  as doubt about the existing evidence.
- Don't ask about next steps *first* — if you must, it goes after THE question,
  one sentence: "And whenever it suits — what does the process look like from
  here?"
- Don't over-thank. One sincere thank-you outperforms three escalating ones.
- Don't say "hopefully see you soon!" Hope is not your brand. Evidence is your
  brand. "Whatever you decide — it's checkable" IS the brand.

## The very last thing (for you, not them)

Outside the building, before the adrenaline crash: one breath, one sentence,
said internally and meant:

*The chain is sealed. Whatever they decide, the work was real.*

Then go eat something. You earned it eighty-two tests ago.
