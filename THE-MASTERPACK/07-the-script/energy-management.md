# Energy Management

*An interview is a two-hour endurance event scored on the last five minutes as
heavily as the first. Energy is a finite resource; this is the budget.*

---

## The night before

- **Do:** one full read of `rapid-fire-deck.md`, one walkthrough of the bundle's
  page order (poster 1, comic 2, ... close 10), pack everything physical (see
  DAY-BEFORE-CHECKLIST), lay out clothes.
- **Don't:** rehearse answers past 9pm (late rehearsal converts knowledge into
  anxiety), read new material (anything you learn tonight will be the thing you
  misquote tomorrow), or "quickly check" the codebase (you will find something to
  fix; you will fix it; you will sleep four hours).
- **Sleep:** the same time as a normal night, not earlier — forcing early sleep
  manufactures the 2am ceiling-stare. Normal time, normal routine.

## The morning of

- Breakfast you've eaten before. The interview morning is not the day to discover
  how your body feels about a triple espresso.
- **Review ONLY the rapid-fire deck** (20 minutes, on the way there). It's
  designed for exactly this — everything else is already in you, and cramming the
  war room at 8am replaces calm knowledge with fresh panic.
- Arrive 15 minutes early. Walk around the block once. The walk is not killing
  time; it's converting cortisol into motion.

## Walking in: the physiological sigh

Before the door: **double inhale through the nose (one full breath, then a short
top-up sniff), then one long exhale through the mouth.** Twice. It's the fastest
known voluntary down-regulation of stress arousal — and it's invisible if you do
it in the corridor, not the doorway.

## During: the budget rules

- **Drink water during THEIR long questions** — never during your own answers
  (mid-answer sips read as stalling).
- **One-second reset after every answer:** exhale, settle, eyes back to the room.
  Without the reset, answers blur into each other and pace compounds until you're
  sprinting. The reset is a comma in the conversation.
- **Spend peak energy on Acts 2 and 5.** The story and the close are scored on
  warmth; warmth is expensive. Technical Q&A (Act 4) runs efficiently on lower
  energy because precision doesn't need wattage.

## The 45-minute dip

It happens. To everyone. Around minute 45, glucose and adrenaline both sag and
your answers will want to shorten.

The protocol: sit up one vertebra taller, plant both feet flat, one slow breath
during their next question — and *deliberately re-expand your next answer* by one
example. The dip lasts about five minutes if you ride it and the whole remaining
hour if you panic about it.

## If energy genuinely crashes

You have a legal move, and it costs nothing:

> "Could I take ten seconds to think about that one properly?"

Said calmly, this is always — always — received as thoughtfulness, not weakness.
No interviewer in history has written "took a moment to think" in the rejection
notes. Use it up to twice; budget it like the timeout it is.

## After (yes, this is part of energy management)

The adrenaline debt arrives ~30 minutes post-interview as a crash and an
overwhelming urge to replay every answer. Plan for it: eat something, walk, and
write down — once — the three questions that surprised you (they feed the next
iteration of the war room). Then stop replaying. The interview is sealed; the
chain doesn't re-open.
