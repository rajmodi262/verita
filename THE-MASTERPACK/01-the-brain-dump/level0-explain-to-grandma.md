# Level 0 — For Grandma

*Audience: someone who asks "what's a website?" Voice: patient, warm, kitchen-table.*

---

Let me tell you a story about a library.

Imagine a little town with a library that keeps a record of every rupee that moves
through the town. Who paid whom, when, how much. Thousands and thousands of slips of
paper, in boxes, on shelves. Somewhere in those boxes, a few slips are *lies* — a thief
moving stolen money and trying to make it look ordinary.

Now, the town hires a librarian whose whole job is to find those lying slips. The poor
librarian! Before she can even *start* looking, she has to spend her whole first day just
sorting the boxes — which slips are payments, which are dates, which are names. Only
then can she begin to actually think.

What I built is a helper for that librarian. You hand it the whole box of slips, and in
the time it takes to pour a cup of tea, it has sorted everything, laid it all out on a
big table where you can see it at a glance, and put little flags on the slips that look
suspicious — "this one's odd, look here first."

But here's the part I'm proudest of, and it's the part I want you to remember.

My helper never says "trust me." Every single time it flags a slip or adds up a column,
you can ask it — *"how do you know that?"* — and it shows you its arithmetic, right
there, like a child showing their working in the margins of their maths homework. And
when it finishes investigating, it seals its whole notebook with a special wax seal. If
anyone — anyone — changes even one word of the notebook afterward, the seal visibly
breaks. So the librarian can hand the notebook to a judge and say: nobody has touched
this. Not even the helper itself.

I built it because banks have this exact problem, except their library has millions of
slips, and the law says they must be able to *prove* why they flagged what they flagged.
A helper that just says "trust me" is useless to them — it could even get them in
trouble. Mine shows its work. That's its whole personality. I named it **Verita** —
from the word for *truth*.

And there's a big company called Wolters Kluwer — they make the tools that banks and
courts and hospitals trust for exactly this kind of careful work. I built my helper the
way *they* would need it built, and now I'm going to show it to them and ask them to
let me come build things with them.

---

**The sentence for the bridge club:**

> "My grandchild built a computer helper that catches money thieves for banks — and it
> shows its homework so the judge believes it."
