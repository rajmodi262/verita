# The Pitch Arsenal — Seven Weapons, One Project

*Different rooms, different ammunition. Practice all seven aloud until each fits in
its time box without hurry.*

---

## 1. THE TWITTER PITCH (280 characters)

> Built an AI fraud investigator that shows its work: every metric carries its
> formula, every agent decision its SQL, the whole reasoning trace sealed in a
> SHA-256 chain. Doctor one step — the chain snaps. Auditable AI for the industry
> where "trust me" is illegal.

*(279 characters. Yes, it was counted.)*

## 2. THE HEADLINE PITCH

> **"CANDIDATE REFUSES TO LET AI LIE; BUILDS THE RECEIPTS IN."**

*(Alternate, broadsheet-sober: "New analytics platform makes every AI decision
re-runnable — regulators could check the math.")*

## 3. THE VC PITCH (30 seconds — they care about money and scale)

> "Financial institutions spend billions on compliance because regulators demand
> defensible decisions — and the AI boom is making decisions *less* defensible.
> Verita inverts that: instant BI from any dataset, fraud scoring with honest
> held-out metrics, and an autonomous investigator whose entire reasoning trace is
> tamper-evident. The moat isn't the model — models commoditize. It's the audit
> layer: provenance infrastructure that turns AI from a compliance risk into a
> compliance asset. Built solo in weeks; imagine it with a team."

## 4. THE TECHNICAL PITCH (60 seconds — they care about the HOW)

> "Three engines on a FastAPI core. One: a profiling engine — semantic type
> inference, quality scoring, scipy-backed insights with real p-values — feeding a
> chart-recommendation layer that composes an editable ECharts dashboard from any
> upload. Two: a scikit-learn risk pipeline — GradientBoosting plus IsolationForest
> on 284k real transactions, held-out ROC-AUC 0.913, PR-AUC 0.65 because prevalence
> is 0.17% and accuracy would be a lie — with a live decision-threshold slider.
> Three, the flagship: a deterministic investigation agent that plans hypotheses
> from the data's shape, tests each with real DuckDB SQL, and serializes every step
> into a SHA-256 hash chain — tamper-evident, re-runnable, regulator-shaped. SQL
> surface is sandboxed at the engine level with an adversarial injection corpus in
> CI — which caught a real vulnerability before shipping. 82 tests. Zero fabricated
> metrics. One-command boot."

## 5. THE IMPACT PITCH (60 seconds — they care about WHY it matters)

> "When an AI flags a transaction as suspicious, a human being gets investigated.
> Maybe their account freezes. Maybe a report with their name goes to a government
> agency. In that world, an AI that can't explain itself isn't a tool — it's an
> unaccountable accuser. That's why regulators require explainability, and it's why
> most AI analytics can't actually be deployed in compliance. Verita is my answer:
> a system where every number can show its formula, every model reports the honest
> error rate, and the AI investigator's entire reasoning is sealed so it can't be
> quietly rewritten. It makes the machine accusable back. I built it for the one
> industry where showing your work isn't pedagogy — it's due process."

## 6. THE PERSONAL PITCH (60 seconds — why ME, not just the project)

> "What you're actually evaluating isn't this project — it's whether the loop that
> produced it repeats. Here's the loop: I read your job description the way an
> analyst reads data — found the real constraint, which is defensibility — and
> instead of writing 'passionate about AI' on a CV, I built the working answer. When
> my own flagship feature turned out to be a black box, I lost a week rebuilding it
> honest, because the thesis matters more than the demo. When my security tests beat
> my own SQL console, I rebuilt the boundary at the engine level and kept the attack
> as a permanent test. That loop — understand deeply, build honestly, attack my own
> claims, show the work — doesn't need supervision to run. It needs harder problems.
> You have the hardest ones."

## 7. THE WOLTERS KLUWER PITCH (their language, their world)

> "Wolters Kluwer's whole position is *expert solutions* — software professionals
> bet their license on. In FSS and FCC, your users defend their decisions to
> auditors, to FinCEN, to courts. So when your JD asks for AI-driven dashboards,
> predictive models, NLP, and GenAI, there's an unwritten requirement under all of
> it: *defensible*. That's the requirement I built against. Verita does the JD's
> surface — the Python stack, the Power-BI-style canvas, forecasting with backtests,
> hypothesis testing, BSA/AML/OFAC-aware NLP — but its spine is the part your
> customers actually buy: every number shows its formula, every model shows its
> held-out error, and the autonomous investigator produces a tamper-evident,
> re-runnable trace. It's a small product with your industry's values built in. I'd
> like to learn how the real ones are made."
