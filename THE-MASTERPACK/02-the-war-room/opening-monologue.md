# The First 90 Seconds — Three Openers

*Not a speech. A conversation opener that takes control of the room. Choose by the
room's energy in the first ten seconds — formal, warm, or neutral — and never blend
two openers. Blended openers read as nerves.*

---

## VERSION A: "The Bold Open"
**Use when:** the room is relaxed-confident, more than one interviewer, at least one
technical person present. **Risk level:** medium-high, highest ceiling.

*[You've placed the bundle on the table. You've sat. Beat. Look at them — not at
the bundle.]*

> "Before we start — I want to be upfront about something. I'm not a traditional
> tech person. I built this project using Claude Code and AI tools — openly, and
> I'll show you exactly where the line between me and the machine sits.
>
> Everything in this room — *[one small gesture at the bundle, eyes stay on them]* —
> came from understanding a real problem deeply enough to build a real solution for
> it: financial crime analytics that a regulator could actually audit.
>
> I think that's more interesting than memorizing algorithms. But you get to judge
> that. Should we start?"

*[The question at the end matters: it hands control back, which is what makes the
boldness land as confidence instead of performance.]*

---

## VERSION B: "The Story Open"
**Use when:** the room is warm, HR-led, or anyone has asked a soft icebreaker.
**Risk level:** low, high floor, builds connection fastest.

> "A few months ago I read what Wolters Kluwer actually does — not the Wikipedia
> version, the real one: your tools sit in front of compliance officers who have to
> defend every flagged transaction to a regulator. And I had this thought I couldn't
> shake: everyone is racing to put AI into finance, and almost nobody is building
> the version a regulator could audit.
>
> I had [X] weeks, an AI assistant, and an unreasonable amount of stubbornness.
> This — *[hand rests on the bundle, once]* — is what happened.
>
> It's called Verita. Every number it shows can show its work. I'd love to walk you
> through it — or you can just start asking the hard questions. Dealer's choice."

*["Dealer's choice" is the disarm — it signals you fear neither path.]*

---

## VERSION C: "The Humble Open"
**Use when:** the room is formal, senior, time-pressed, or one-on-one with someone
unreadable. **Risk level:** lowest, never backfires.

> "I built something specifically for this interview. Not because I assumed it would
> impress you — though I hope it does — but because I wanted to show you how I think
> about problems, and a document does that more honestly than adjectives.
>
> *[Slide the bundle across — the only time the bundle moves in any opener.]*
>
> The short version: it's a working financial-crime analytics platform, built
> against your team's job description, where every number can show its formula and
> the AI's investigation is sealed against tampering.
>
> Everything I'd want to say is in there. I'd love to walk you through it — if
> you're open to that."

*[Ending on their permission is the humility making itself useful: whatever they
answer, they've now chosen to engage with the bundle.]*

---

## SELECTION RULES (decide in the first ten seconds)

| Room signal | Open with |
|---|---|
| They joke, offer coffee, lean back | **A** — boldness matches their comfort |
| They ask about your journey/day first | **B** — they invited a story; tell the story |
| Formal greetings, visible schedule pressure, unreadable | **C** — earn the room before you play in it |
| Panel with mixed signals | **B** — the story is legible at every level |

**One rule above all:** whichever opener you choose, the last sentence ends with the
decision in THEIR hands ("Should we start?" / "Dealer's choice." / "If you're open
to that."). Control of the room is demonstrated by giving it away on your own terms.
