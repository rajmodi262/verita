# If Everything Goes Wrong

*Contingency scripts. Read once now, once the day before. The goal isn't to avoid
disasters — it's to have already met them, so the room watches you handle one
calmly and hires the handling.*

---

## SCENARIO: My mind goes completely blank

**Script:** "I want to give you a real answer, not a rushed one — let me take a
second."
[Look at the relevant page of your own bundle. Yes, openly. You built an external
brain for exactly this moment.]
[Find the anchor — a diagram, a number — and answer from there.]

**Why it works:** consulting your own documentation under pressure doesn't read as
forgetting; it reads as *a person with documentation*. In this industry that's a
virtue bordering on a fetish.

## SCENARIO: They ask something I truly don't know

**Script:** "I don't have direct experience with that — but here's how I'd reason
about it from first principles..." [reason aloud, genuinely] "...I'd want to verify
that before acting on it. Is that something you run into often here?"

**Never:** bluff. The building detects bluffs professionally. A clean "here's my
reasoning, here's my uncertainty" is the senior move — it's literally the product's
philosophy (confidence reported, never faked) performed live.

## SCENARIO: They seem unimpressed / bored

[Stop explaining. Mid-sentence if necessary — finish the clause, drop the rest.]

**Script:** "I'm curious — is this the kind of problem your team actually works
on? I'd rather spend our time on what's relevant to you."

**Why it works:** boredom usually means wrong altitude, not wrong candidate. The
question lets them steer, and the *willingness to be steered* is itself the signal
they were missing.

## SCENARIO: They challenge something and they're right

**Script:** "You're right — I had that wrong." [correct it, cleanly, no qualifiers]
"That's genuinely useful — it's going on my list. How do you handle that in your
current system?"

**Why it works:** the fastest possible concession plus a curious counter-question
converts a scored point against you into a conversation between colleagues.
Defensiveness converts it into a verdict. (And one wrong fact, corrected
gracefully, outperforms zero wrong facts delivered anxiously.)

## SCENARIO: They ask about something the project doesn't do

**Script:** "That's V2." [smile] "In the current version I focused on [the
relevant pillar] because [the reason]. If I had another month, that's first — and
honestly, the engines for it are already installed."

**Why it works:** scope cuts with reasons are engineering; scope cuts with
apologies are gaps. You wrote the limits down before they asked — point at the
page where you did.

## SCENARIO: The printed bundle tears / printer failed / I forgot it

[Everything is also on your phone as PDFs, because the day-before checklist said
so.]

**Script:** "I have the digital version — I'll send it to you right after this.
Let me walk you through it anyway."
[Then present from memory. You can — the page order is in your spine by now:
poster, comic, problem/solution, advantage, journey, constellations, homework,
numbers, next, close.]

**Why it works:** materials failing and the candidate not failing with them is —
inconveniently but truly — a better demonstration than the materials themselves.

## SCENARIO: The live demo breaks in front of everyone

**Script:** "Well — this is exactly why the product has error handling." [one
beat for the laugh] "Let me show you what it just did instead of crashing."
[Show the graceful failure: the clean error, the health endpoint, the fallback
mode. Narrate it as a feature, because it is one.]

**Fallback if truly dead:** the screenshots in the README and the bundle ARE the
demo. "The repo's open — I'd rather you run it on your own machine anyway. It's
one click."

**Why it works:** you built a product whose thesis is honest failure. A demo
failing honestly is on-brand in a way no slide could ever be.

## SCENARIO: Hostile interviewer, sustained

[Recognize it: some panels assign one skeptic deliberately.]

Rules: answer the question, never the tone. Slower, not faster. Keep your answers
the same length as for a friendly asker — shrinking answers reward the pressure.
Deploy evidence, page numbers, and the standing offer ("test it — pick any layer").
And remember the meta-game: the skeptic's report is often the one that says
"pushed them hard; they held."

---

## The master contingency

Whatever breaks — tech, memory, paper, room — the unbreakable asset walks in with
you: **you actually built the thing, and you actually understand it.** Every script
above is just a different route back to that fact. The materials are evidence; you
are the witness. Witnesses who stay calm get believed.
