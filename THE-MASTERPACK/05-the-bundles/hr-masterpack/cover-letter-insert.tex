Dear \TheCompany\ team,

Most cover letters describe what the candidate would do if hired. I'd rather show
you what I already did because you might hire me.

When I read your posting for the \TheTeam\ internship, one phrase under the surface
of every bullet caught me: your users must \emph{defend} their numbers — to auditors,
to regulators, to courts. Every company is adding AI right now. Almost nobody is
building the version a compliance officer could take into that room.

So I spent \BuildWeeks\ weeks building it. \textbf{\ProjectName} turns any dataset
into an editable, BI-grade dashboard in seconds; scores transactions with a fraud
model whose metrics are honestly held-out (ROC-AUC \StatRocAuc\ on \StatTxns\ real
transactions — and it tells you so on screen); and runs an autonomous investigator
whose every step — hypothesis, SQL query, statistical verdict — is sealed into a
SHA-256 chain. Alter one step afterward and the chain visibly breaks. Every number
in the product can show its formula. There are exactly \StatFabricated\ fabricated
metrics in it, enforced by \StatTests\ automated tests in CI.

I built it with AI tooling, openly — reviewed line by line, verified claim by
claim, with the division of labor documented in the technical bundle. I believe
that's not a caveat but a preview: your industry's next decade is AI adoption
without trust erosion, and I've been practicing exactly that discipline, alone, on
purpose, for you.

What I want from this internship is the part I can't self-teach: production scale,
real institutional data, and the standards of people who've defended models to
regulators. What you get is someone who arrives already running — and who treats
“show your work” as a way of life, not a feature.

The full evidence is attached: a working product, its documentation, and the
presentation materials you're holding. I'd welcome the chance to be interrogated
about any of it, at any depth.

\vspace{4mm}
With respect and a working demo,
